# Accessible beta report process

## Purpose

The beta report is a conversation tool for small-business owners, not a technical audit presented without explanation. It should remain evidence-backed and reproducible while using direct, everyday language.

## 1. Owner conversation before testing

Ask the owner:

1. What should your business be best known for?
2. Which services or products matter most commercially?
3. What might an ideal customer ask when looking for those services?
4. Which locations or areas matter?
5. Are there particular customer types, needs or occasions you want to attract?
6. Which businesses do you consider competitors?
7. Is there anything you do not want to promote?
8. What would a useful improvement look like in three months?

Record owner-provided answers separately from anything inferred from the website or category. Confirm the priority services and proposed questions with the owner before running a paid benchmark.

## 2. Build the question set

Create two clearly labelled groups:

- Owner-priority questions: based directly on the owner's goals and preferred customer language.
- Supplementary questions: generated from the business category, location and observable proposition to broaden the diagnostic.

Keep every final question verbatim in the evidence payload. Disclose the platforms, models, repetitions and audit date.

## 3. Measure and compare

Run the approved questions across the selected AI platforms. Count only valid completed responses. Reconcile named businesses to their canonical Google Place IDs and retain unmatched or non-business items according to the established evidence rules.

Compare:

- businesses nominated by the owner;
- businesses that actually appear most often in the measured answers; and
- the target business's website and customer-review evidence.

Do not imply that an observed competitor difference caused an AI recommendation.

## 4. Select customer quotations

Use a small number of exact quotations from the frozen review sets to illustrate wider evidence patterns.

- Preserve the original wording and punctuation.
- Store the review ID and Google Place ID with every quotation.
- Validate the displayed quotation against the frozen record during rendering.
- Do not include reviewer names in the client report.
- Label target-business and comparison-business quotes clearly.
- Do not treat one quotation as representative when it conflicts with the complete review set.

## 5. Write for the owner

Use the business name, "your business" and "your website". Avoid internal labels such as "target", "corpus", "provider breadth", "entity resolution" and "frozen benchmark" in the main story.

Lead each section with context. Prefer:

- What we did
- What we found
- What this means for your business
- What others show more clearly
- What you can do
- The practical benefit

Keep technical provenance, hashes, exclusions and limitations in the methodology appendix unless a detail materially changes the interpretation.

## 6. Present and collect frank feedback

Treat the PDF as a discussion draft. During the beta conversation, ask:

- Did the report reflect what you want to be known for?
- Were the customer questions realistic?
- Which finding felt most useful or surprising?
- Was anything unclear, too technical or too absolute?
- Did the customer quotations feel fair and recognisable?
- Which recommended action feels achievable?
- What information would you need before acting?

Record feedback against the report version. Separate corrections to evidence from preferences about tone or presentation.

## 7. Approval and remeasurement

Keep a report in `review_draft` status while it is being discussed. Operator approval is required before freezing a snapshot. After meaningful implementation, repeat the same owner-priority questions and disclose any platform, model or methodology changes.
